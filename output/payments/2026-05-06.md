**New York introduces first US state-level open banking legislation**

*A proposed New York bill would create the first state-level open banking-style data access regime in the US, adding a new regulatory layer alongside the federal CFPB rule currently facing legal challenge.*

New York State has introduced legislation that would grant consumers the right to share their financial data with authorised third parties, mirroring the structure of open banking frameworks already established in the UK and EU. If enacted, it would be the first such regime at state level in the US.

The timing is significant. The CFPB's federal open banking rule, finalised under the Biden administration, faces ongoing legal challenges and an uncertain future under the current White House. A state-level regime in the country's largest financial centre would create a parallel compliance obligation for banks and fintechs operating in New York, regardless of the federal rule's fate.

For European open banking providers eyeing the US market, New York's move signals that data-sharing mandates are advancing through multiple channels. It also raises the prospect of a fragmented US regulatory landscape, where institutions must navigate both federal and state-level requirements, a dynamic familiar to those tracking data protection law in the US.

*Editorial note: A state-level open banking regime in New York is a material development for any European firm with US exposure, and illustrates how open banking policy is evolving differently across jurisdictions.*

**FCA sandbox applications rose 49% in 2025, driven by AI and open banking**

*Applications to the FCA's Regulatory Sandbox and Innovation Pathways jumped sharply last year, with open banking, open finance and AI among the dominant use cases.*

A regulatory round-up covering the week ending April 24 highlights that applications to the FCA's sandbox and innovation programmes rose 49% in 2025 compared with the prior year. AI, distributed ledger technology, open banking and open finance were the main technologies cited by applicants.

The FCA has indicated that during 2026 it will focus on clearer testing criteria and broader engagement with applicants. The surge in applications suggests that firms are actively seeking regulatory guidance ahead of the UK's anticipated open finance framework, rather than waiting for final rules. It also reflects the degree to which AI-powered financial services are now a mainstream regulatory concern, not a fringe experiment.

For European firms, the data point is a useful benchmark. The FCA's sandbox has long been a bellwether for where innovation pressure is building. A near-50% rise in applications, concentrated in open banking and AI, confirms that product development is running ahead of regulation in these areas.

*Editorial note: The sharp rise in FCA sandbox applications is a concrete indicator of where UK fintech innovation is concentrating, and signals regulatory demand that will shape the open finance framework.*

**HM Treasury payments package sets direction for UK payment services modernisation**

*Freshfields has published analysis of HM Treasury's new package to modernise UK payment services, which sits alongside the FCA's open finance vision published in April.*

Law firm Freshfields has set out the implications of HM Treasury's recently announced package to modernise UK payment services regulation. The package, which follows HM Treasury's earlier consultations, is designed to update the UK's post-Brexit payments framework and sits alongside the FCA's open finance roadmap published on April 14.

The FCA's roadmap, titled "Open finance: Our vision for a smart data future", laid out the regulator's plans for expanding data-sharing beyond payments accounts into areas such as insurance, pensions and investments. HM Treasury's complementary package addresses the broader regulatory architecture for payment services, including authorisation, supervision and consumer protection.

Taken together, the two publications represent the most detailed articulation yet of how the UK intends to diverge from the EU's PSD2/PSD3 trajectory. The direction is clear: the UK is pursuing a broader open finance mandate while simultaneously overhauling its payments regulation. Firms operating across both jurisdictions will need to manage increasingly distinct compliance obligations.

*Editorial note: The convergence of HM Treasury's payments package and the FCA's open finance vision represents a defining moment in UK payments policy divergence from the EU.*

**Montenegro's central bank connects to European instant payments infrastructure**

*The Central Bank of Montenegro has joined Europe's instant payments infrastructure, advancing the country's EU integration agenda through payments interoperability.*

The governor of the Central Bank of Montenegro (CBCG), Radović, has stated that the institution is delivering concrete results in the European integration process, citing its connection to European instant payments infrastructure. The move positions Montenegro alongside EU member states in payments interoperability, despite the country still being in accession negotiations.

For the broader European instant payments landscape, Montenegro's connection is part of a pattern of non-EU countries aligning with EU payment rails ahead of formal membership. It reflects the gravitational pull of the EU's instant payments regulation, which is driving harmonisation well beyond the bloc's current borders.

The practical significance is modest in volume terms, but the signal matters: European instant payments infrastructure is becoming a de facto standard that candidate countries adopt proactively, rather than waiting for accession requirements to compel it.

*Editorial note: Montenegro's early adoption of EU instant payments infrastructure illustrates how the regulation's reach extends beyond member states, a dynamic worth tracking as the October 2025 deadline effects continue to play out.*
