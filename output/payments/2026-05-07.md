**FCA opens competition probe into Mastercard, Visa and PayPal over digital wallets**

*The FCA and Payment Systems Regulator have launched a formal anti-competition investigation into the three dominant digital wallet providers, signalling regulatory intent to open up a fast-growing segment of UK payments.*

The FCA announced on May 6 that it is investigating suspected anti-competitive arrangements involving Mastercard, Visa and PayPal in the digital wallet market. The probe follows a joint FCA–PSR report published last year that flagged competition concerns as digital wallets rose to 29% of UK card transactions in 2023. The investigation will examine whether the three firms' commercial arrangements are restricting competition in ways that harm consumers and merchants.

The move marks a significant escalation. Where the earlier report identified structural concerns, a formal investigation carries enforcement powers and the prospect of remedies. For the payments industry, the probe raises questions about the commercial terms that underpin wallet acceptance, including scheme fee structures and merchant routing constraints.

The timing is notable given the FCA's expanded remit following the PSR's integration. Digital wallets sit at the intersection of card scheme economics and consumer payment habits, and a competition intervention here could reshape the terms on which wallets compete with emerging alternatives such as account-to-account payments.

*Editorial note: This is the most consequential regulatory action in today's feed — a formal competition investigation into the three largest wallet providers has direct implications for scheme economics, merchant pricing and alternative payment methods across the UK.*

**Open Banking Limited appoints former FCA general counsel to board**

*The appointment reinforces OBL's legal and regulatory capacity as the UK prepares to extend open banking principles into open finance.*

Open Banking Limited has appointed a former FCA general counsel to its board. The move comes shortly after the UK government published its Smart Data Strategy and the FCA released its open finance roadmap, both of which set out plans to expand regulated data sharing beyond payments into areas such as insurance, pensions and investments.

OBL's mandate is evolving from overseeing the CMA's original open banking remedy into stewarding a broader open finance framework. Board-level regulatory expertise becomes more important as OBL navigates this transition, particularly given the need to align with FCA expectations on governance, liability and data standards across new sectors.

*Editorial note: A board appointment at OBL would normally be routine, but the regulatory context — the Smart Data Strategy and FCA open finance roadmap — makes the choice of a former FCA general counsel substantively significant.*

**HES FinTech and Acquired.com bring commercial variable recurring payments to lending**

*The integration of Visa's account-to-account commercial variable recurring payments into a lending platform signals that cVRPs are moving beyond early-adopter use cases into mainstream financial product infrastructure.*

HES FinTech has expanded its partnership with Acquired.com to embed commercial variable recurring payments into HES LoanBox, its lending-as-a-service platform. The integration uses Visa's account-to-account rails and is available as a native, out-of-the-box capability within the platform, sitting alongside cards, direct debit and open banking payment methods.

The significance lies less in the partnership itself than in what it reveals about cVRP adoption. Recurring loan repayments are a high-value, high-frequency use case where cVRPs offer clear advantages over direct debit: real-time confirmation, lower failure rates and consent granularity. Acquired.com's approach of unifying multiple payment methods within a single infrastructure reflects an emerging pattern where cVRPs complement rather than replace existing rails.

For the broader UK market, this is evidence that cVRPs are beginning to find their way into production systems beyond the initial retail and subscription contexts. Lending is a natural next frontier, though scalability will depend on continued expansion of bank coverage and standardised consent flows.

*Editorial note: cVRP adoption in lending infrastructure is a meaningful development for the UK's variable recurring payments rollout, showing the capability moving into more complex financial products.*

**CBI posts 14% revenue rise as payment and open finance services grow**

*The Dutch payments infrastructure provider's revenue growth reflects expanding demand for instant payment and open finance capabilities among European financial institutions.*

CBI reported a 14% increase in revenue, driven by growth in its payment services and a broadening role in open finance infrastructure. The company serves more than 400 financial institutions and has been expanding its services as European banks respond to instant payment mandates and open finance requirements.

The result is a useful indicator of where infrastructure spending is flowing. CBI sits in the plumbing layer of European payments, and double-digit revenue growth there reflects the operational investment that banks are making to meet regulatory deadlines and competitive pressures. The company's push into open finance services beyond core payments processing aligns with a wider pattern of infrastructure providers extending their propositions as the regulatory perimeter expands.

*Editorial note: CBI's revenue figures offer a concrete, quantitative signal of how European demand for instant payment and open finance infrastructure is translating into actual spending.*
