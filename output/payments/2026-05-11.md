**Keel pivots from neobank to fintech infrastructure play**

*A UK-based startup has abandoned its consumer banking ambitions to offer multi-rail payment and open banking infrastructure to other fintechs, joining a crowded but consolidating market.*

Keel has relaunched as a fintech infrastructure provider after pivoting away from its original neobank model. The company now offers open banking services alongside access to domestic and international payment rails such as Faster Payments, BACS, CHAPS, SEPA, SWIFT, ACH and Fedwire. The breadth of rail coverage positions it as a one-stop connectivity layer for fintechs needing cross-border reach without assembling their own patchwork of integrations.

The pivot follows a well-worn path. Several European startups that launched with consumer or SME banking propositions have reoriented toward infrastructure as the economics of neobanking have proven difficult and demand for banking-as-a-service and payment connectivity has grown. The question for Keel is differentiation: the embedded finance infrastructure space in Europe is mature, with established players such as Modulr, ClearBank and Banking Circle already competing for the same clients. Whether Keel's specific combination of rails and open banking capability gives it a defensible position remains to be seen.

*Editorial note: The neobank-to-infrastructure pivot is a recurring pattern worth tracking. It signals both the difficulty of building sustainable consumer fintech businesses and the persistent demand for multi-rail connectivity in European payments.*

**Albania moves toward EU payments alignment with instant payments push**

*Albania's payments modernisation programme, including instant payments infrastructure and open banking foundations, offers a preview of how EU accession candidates are reshaping their financial systems.*

Albania is advancing from basic digitisation toward structural alignment with EU payment standards, according to a landscape review published by The Fintech Times. The country's instant payments push is described as one of the clearest signals of its direction, alongside early-stage work on open banking and faster payments infrastructure.

For European payments professionals, the significance lies less in Albania's domestic market size than in the pattern it represents. EU candidate countries across the Western Balkans are building payments infrastructure that converges with SEPA and PSD2/PSD3 norms well ahead of formal accession. This creates both a future expansion of the single payments area and near-term opportunities for infrastructure providers, processors and fintechs looking to establish early presence in markets that will eventually fall under EU regulatory frameworks.

*Editorial note: Albania is a minor market on its own, but the broader story of pre-accession payments convergence across the Western Balkans is material for anyone planning European expansion strategies.*

**Australian banks and fintechs head for standoff over open banking reforms**

*A brewing conflict between Australian banks and fintechs over proposed open banking reforms carries lessons for European markets navigating similar tensions around data sharing obligations.*

Australian banks appear to be engaging in brinkmanship over proposed reforms to the Consumer Data Right, with FinTech Australia and the banking sector set for another confrontation, Capital Brief reports. Separately, the Australian Banking Association has called for inclusion of tax office data in the CDR framework, while questioning the costs, usage and utility of open banking nearly six years after it went live in the country.

The ABA's framing is notable. By simultaneously advocating for CDR expansion into tax data and questioning open banking's return on investment, the banking lobby is positioning itself to shape which data flows into the system and on what terms. The Australian experience is watched closely in Europe, where PSD3 and the Financial Data Access regulation will create analogous dynamics. The core tension, who bears the cost of data-sharing infrastructure and who captures the value, is universal. Australia's difficulty in driving consumer adoption despite years of regulatory effort reinforces the European debate about whether mandated access alone is sufficient or whether commercial incentives need to be embedded into the framework.

*Editorial note: Australia's open banking trajectory offers a useful reference point for European policymakers and market participants as FIDA negotiations progress. The persistence of low adoption despite regulatory mandates is a cautionary signal.*

**Real-time payments shift from speed story to cash flow management tool**

*A new tracker from PYMNTS Intelligence argues that the future of instant payments depends not on transaction speed but on embedding them into everyday cash flow decisions, a framing with direct relevance to European account-to-account payment strategies.*

The April 2026 Real-Time Payments Tracker, produced with The Clearing House, contends that real-time payments are transitioning from novelty to necessity but face an adoption challenge: making them habitual rather than occasional. The report positions cash flow management, not speed, as the primary value proposition that will drive sustained consumer and business use.

While the tracker is US-focused, the argument resonates in Europe, where the instant payments regulation has mandated SEPA instant credit transfer availability but commercial uptake remains uneven. The European challenge mirrors the one described: infrastructure availability has outpaced use-case development. Pay-by-bank and account-to-account payment advocates in Europe have largely emphasised cost savings over cards, but the cash flow framing suggests a more compelling angle, particularly for SME payments, payroll and gig economy disbursements where timing of funds matters as much as the transaction itself.

*Editorial note: The reframing of instant payments around cash flow rather than speed is relevant to European strategists trying to build commercial propositions on top of mandated infrastructure.*
