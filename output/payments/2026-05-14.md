**UK Financial Services Bill to fold PSR into FCA and legislate open finance**

*The King's Speech confirmed legislation to merge the Payment Systems Regulator into the Financial Conduct Authority and establish a statutory framework for open finance, the most significant restructuring of UK payments oversight in a decade.*

The Enhancing Financial Services Bill, outlined in yesterday's King's Speech, will consolidate the PSR within the FCA, ending the current fragmented supervisory model for payments. The government framed the move as reducing regulatory duplication for firms, though the British Retail Consortium was quick to warn that the FCA must continue the PSR's work on card scheme fees. No timetable for the Bill has been published; much of the detail will depend on secondary legislation and FCA rulemaking.

The Bill also signals the government's intent to put open finance on a statutory footing. The FCA had previously published a roadmap for extending data-sharing beyond payments accounts, and the speech confirmed that legislation would follow. For firms operating across payments, banking and investment, the practical consequence is a single regulatory counterpart for both conduct and payment systems oversight, though the transition will take years to complete.

Retailers and payment processors should watch the detail on how the PSR's existing functions, particularly its enforcement powers on interchange and scheme fees, transfer into the FCA's broader mandate. There is a real risk that payments-specific priorities get diluted within a larger regulator.

*Editorial note: The PSR-FCA merger and open finance legislation are the most consequential UK regulatory developments for payments professionals this year, directly reshaping the oversight landscape.*

**Sixth Circuit pauses challenges to CFPB open banking rule as agency rewrites it**

*The US appeals court handling industry challenges to the CFPB's Section 1033 rule has suspended proceedings while the bureau undertakes a substantial revision, leaving the US open banking framework in limbo.*

The Sixth Circuit granted a stay requested by plaintiffs, including banking trade groups, after the CFPB indicated it intends to conduct a new rulemaking that would substantially revise the open banking rule finalised under the Biden administration. The pause effectively freezes the legal battle while the rule is rewritten, meaning compliance deadlines remain uncertain for large US banks that were first in line.

For European firms with US operations or cross-border data-sharing ambitions, the delay widens the gap between the EU's Financial Data Access framework and any comparable US regime. It also provides a case study in how a change of administration can stall data-sharing regulation even after final rules are published, a dynamic that has no direct parallel in the EU but illustrates the political fragility of open finance mandates.

*Editorial note: This is a material development in the US open banking timeline with direct read-across for European firms operating transatlantically and for policymakers watching implementation risks.*

**Moneyline deploys variable recurring payments for social lending repayments**

*A UK community lender is using NatWest's Payit service to offer variable recurring payments for loan repayments, one of the first live applications of VRP in social lending.*

East Lancashire Moneyline, a non-profit lender serving borrowers excluded from mainstream credit, has partnered with NatWest's open banking business Payit to enable flexible loan repayments via variable recurring payments. The integration allows borrowers to adjust repayment amounts within agreed parameters, replacing the rigidity of direct debits for a customer base where income can be irregular.

The deployment is modest in scale but significant as a proof point. VRP has struggled to move beyond sweeping use cases since its introduction, and the commercial VRP framework has been slow to gain traction with merchants and billers. A social lender adopting VRP for recurring debt repayment demonstrates a use case where the technology's flexibility addresses a genuine consumer need rather than simply replicating existing payment methods. It also extends NatWest's Payit into a segment, non-profit lending, that few open banking providers have targeted.

*Editorial note: VRP adoption beyond sweeping remains a closely watched indicator for the open banking payments market; this is a tangible new use case in an underserved segment.*

**EU investor data-sharing proposal extends open finance logic to capital markets**

*An Oxford academic analysis highlights how the European Commission's planned open finance framework would apply data-sharing obligations to investment accounts, not just payments, with implications for wealth platforms and asset managers.*

A post on the Oxford Business Law Blog examines the Commission's intent to extend the open finance framework into investor data-sharing as part of its Digital Finance Strategy. The proposal would allow consumers to share investment portfolio data with authorised third parties, paralleling the account information services model in payments but applied to securities holdings, pension pots and insurance products.

The practical effect would be to enable comparison and switching services for investment products, something that has been technically possible but commercially difficult without standardised data access. For wealth management platforms, robo-advisers and banks with investment arms, this creates both opportunity and competitive exposure. The proposal sits within the broader Financial Data Access regulation, which remains under negotiation, but the direction of travel is now clear: the data-sharing logic that began with payment accounts is being systematically extended across financial services.

*Editorial note: This is an important signal on the scope of EU open finance, moving the conversation beyond payments and banking into capital markets territory that affects a different set of industry players.*
