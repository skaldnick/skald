**CFIT publishes Open Property roadmap, extending smart data beyond financial services**

*The UK's Centre for Finance, Innovation and Technology has set out a concrete plan to apply open banking-style data sharing to the homebuying process, marking the first major expansion of smart data principles into a non-financial sector.*

CFIT's Open Property roadmap, published today and backed by the Department for Business and Trade, lays out how consent-driven, interoperable data sharing could reduce friction in UK property transactions. The coalition behind it includes participants from across financial services, property and conveyancing, and the roadmap draws explicitly on the infrastructure and governance lessons of open banking and open finance.

The significance is less about property itself than about what it signals for the trajectory of smart data policy in the UK. With the Smart Data provisions of the Data Protection and Digital Information Act now in force, CFIT's work provides the first detailed sectoral blueprint beyond financial services. For open banking and open finance participants, this matters in two ways: it validates the model as exportable and creates potential demand for the same identity verification, consent management and API infrastructure they already operate.

Whether the roadmap translates into binding standards or mandated participation remains to be seen. CFIT describes this as the end of "phase one", with implementation dependent on continued government and industry support.

*Editorial note: This is the most substantive UK smart data development today, directly relevant to the open banking ecosystem's strategic direction and the question of whether open data models will scale beyond finance.*

**Bank of England consults on extending CHAPS settlement toward near 24/7 operation**

*The Bank of England has opened a consultation on phased extension of RTGS and CHAPS settlement hours, a move that would bring the UK's high-value payment system closer to round-the-clock availability.*

The consultation sets out a roadmap rather than a single implementation date, reflecting the operational complexity of extending settlement windows for a system that underpins interbank, wholesale and housing-market payments. The phased approach suggests the Bank is mindful of the costs to participants, who would need to staff operations, manage liquidity and maintain resilience across longer windows.

This sits within a broader global trend. The Federal Reserve extended Fedwire hours in 2024, and the ECB has been exploring extended TARGET hours. For UK payment service providers and banks, the practical implications depend heavily on how the phases are structured, particularly whether extended hours will be mandatory or optional for direct participants, and how liquidity management expectations will shift.

The consultation also has knock-on implications for the UK's instant payments infrastructure. Faster Payments already operates 24/7, but the settlement layer beneath it does not. Aligning CHAPS hours more closely with real-time retail payment flows could reduce settlement risk and simplify the overnight liquidity picture for banks operating across both systems.

*Editorial note: A significant infrastructure consultation from the Bank of England with direct operational implications for UK banks and payment firms, and relevant context for the broader push toward always-on settlement globally.*

**BLIK joins EuroPA and EPI in pan-European payment interoperability push**

*Poland's dominant mobile payment scheme has joined the EuroPA association alongside EPI, adding another national instant payment network to the coalition building cross-border account-to-account interoperability in Europe.*

BLIK's participation is notable because of its scale and penetration in Poland, where it processes the majority of mobile payments and has become the default consumer payment method for e-commerce. Its inclusion in EuroPA alongside EPI's wero platform strengthens the case that pan-European account-to-account payments will be built through interoperability between strong national schemes rather than displacement by a single new entrant.

Rollout is expected from 2026. For merchants and payment processors, the practical question is whether this coalition can deliver a checkout experience that competes credibly with card networks on reach and conversion. BLIK's track record in Poland, where it has achieved genuine consumer habit formation, gives it more credibility than many of the schemes that have attempted pan-European expansion. The challenge remains governance, technical harmonisation and the commercial terms that will determine whether acquirers and merchants see sufficient incentive to integrate.

*Editorial note: This advances a continuing story about European payment sovereignty and account-to-account interoperability, with a concrete new participant that materially changes the coalition's weight.*

**FCA and Bank of England warn firms to prepare defences against AI-driven cyber attacks**

*UK financial regulators have issued a joint statement with the Treasury urging firms to strengthen protections against AI-enabled threats to cyber resilience, with particular attention to risks in connected financial infrastructure.*

The joint statement from the FCA, Bank of England and Treasury focuses on the risks posed by frontier AI models being used to enhance cyber attacks against financial institutions. The warning is notable for its specificity: it addresses not just general cyber hygiene but the particular vulnerabilities created by interconnected systems such as open banking networks, where API-based access creates a larger and more dynamic attack surface.

For payments and open banking participants, this is a regulatory signal worth tracking. The statement stops short of imposing new requirements but makes clear that supervisors expect firms to demonstrate they have assessed AI-specific threat vectors and adapted their controls accordingly. Given that the FCA is simultaneously advancing open finance policy, the tension between expanding data access and hardening security will become an increasingly prominent theme in supervisory engagement.

*Editorial note: A regulatory signal from all three UK financial authorities on a topic — AI-driven threats to connected financial infrastructure — that directly intersects with open banking security and is likely to shape supervisory expectations in the near term.*
