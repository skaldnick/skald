**Nationwide introduces in-app controls to block abusive payment references**

*The UK building society has launched a feature letting abuse survivors block payment messages containing harmful content, addressing a specific gap in how payment systems handle economic abuse.*

Nationwide has released an in-app tool that gives customers the ability to block payment reference messages from named senders. The feature targets a known vulnerability in domestic payments: perpetrators of economic abuse use payment reference fields to send threatening, controlling or distressing messages to victims. Because references are freeform text, they have been difficult to police systematically.

The move follows years of campaigning by organisations such as Surviving Economic Abuse and builds on the Payments Systems Regulator's focus on protecting vulnerable customers. While several UK banks have introduced broader measures against economic abuse, Nationwide's approach is notable for giving the customer direct control over blocking, rather than relying solely on back-office monitoring.

For the wider industry, this highlights an area where payment system design has real-world safeguarding consequences. As account-to-account payments grow in volume, the reference field becomes a more frequently used communication channel, making the case for structured controls stronger. Other providers will face pressure to offer similar functionality.

*Editorial note: A practical innovation at the intersection of payments infrastructure and consumer protection, with implications for how the industry handles freeform data fields in real-time payments.*
